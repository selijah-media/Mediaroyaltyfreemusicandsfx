BEFORE YOU DOWNLOAD

Student guide: using music and SFX legally

Important for SAT films

Your film might be shown in class, uploaded online or entered in a film festival. Permission for one use may not cover another. Check the licence for the exact track and keep a copy of its terms. Do not use paid tracks or audio without clear reuse permission. Even correctly licensed music can trigger an automated Content ID claim, so keep your evidence.

01

Free is not copyright-free

“Royalty-free” describes a licensing arrangement; it does not always mean no cost. Some websites mix free and paid tracks. Choose only tracks explicitly available for free use. Being able to listen to or download a song does not automatically mean you can put it in your film.

02

Understand the licence

CC means Creative Commons. BY means credit the creator. NC means NonCommercial: use mainly intended to make money or gain commercial advantage is not automatically allowed. A sponsored film or monetised upload may need separate permission. Check the conditions before publishing.

03

Keep your evidence

Record the track title, creator, original URL, licence and download date. Save a screenshot or copy of the licence in your SAT production documentation. If there are restrictions on editing, sharing or distribution, record those too.

04

Credit correctly

Follow the attribution instructions for the individual track. Where required, include its title, creator, source and licence, plus any changes you made. Put the credit in your film's end credits or online description as directed. Giving credit alone does not replace permission.
